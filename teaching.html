Teaching & Outreach

Teaching philosophy

Teaching is a pillar of my career. I have served as instructor of record five times at the undergraduate and graduate levels, as a teaching assistant for four additional courses, and as the primary mentor to more than a dozen undergraduate and graduate researchers. in 2024 I received a Mentorship Award from the Yale Office for Postdoctoral Affairs. My teaching philosophy has been shaped as much by my own path into science as by my formal training in pedagogy: I came to biology from the arts, a late-declaring major who arrived in my first ecology courses feeling behind in math and out of place among classmates with years of head start in chemistry and biology. That experience gives me real empathy for students who enter my classroom uncertain whether they belong there, and it grounds me in my three core teaching strategies:
First, there is no one right way to be a scientist.
Second, the best scientific education is one cross-pollinated with expertise from other fields.
Third, teaching is one of the best opportunities to generate impact as a scientific researcher; it is always worth investing time and energy into becoming an effective educator.

Courses

Introduction to Ecology for Environmental Managers: Ecological Pattern and Process (ENV511)

Yale School of the Environment · Fall 2023 · Role: Instructor

A fundamental environmental concern is whether natural ecosystems are resilient to natural or human-induced disturbances and sustain their functions. Ecology, as a scientific discipline, offers key theory, tools and empirical insights that can help answer important questions about ecosystem sustainability. This course will provide students with the necessary concepts and tools to begin evaluating how ecological systems are structured and how they respond to natural and human-induced perturbations.

EEMB120 — Introduction to Ecology

UC Santa Barbara · Summer 2020; Spring 2021; Summer 2021 · Role: Instructor

A foundational upper-division undergraduate course in ecology, introducing critical theory, how to conduct ecological research, and how to apply ecology to real-world settings. Starting at the organismal level and ending at the biosphere, we will step through each order of ecological organization on our way toward building understanding of the 'tangled web' that is the environment.

Mentoring

I'm committed to supporting students from a range of backgrounds in research. Past mentees have pursued careers in ecological research; conservation policy; teaching; and more.

Supporting students’ and colleagues’ individual needs are invariably shaped by lived experience and identity. I am extremely aware of the aspects of my identity that have influenced (both positively and negatively) my experiences in science. I work hard to support and validate identities that are not my own.
