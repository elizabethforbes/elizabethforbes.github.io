Research

How do ecological communities respond to environmental change — and what does that mean for the people who depend on them?

I use a combination of long-term field observation, manipulative experiments, and quantitative modeling to ask how ecosytems respond to change in the animal communities that inhabit them, whether that change is through species loss, species introduction, or the addition or subtraction of domestic animals. I'm particularly interested in how changes to an animal community interact with ecosystem structure, and thus its function -- like carbon cycling, biodiversity, and the networks of interactions between animal species. My work has taken me to tropical savannas and atolls, boreal and temperate forests, agricultural landscapes, and marine intertidal zones.

Current projects

The zoogeochemistry of carbon

2020–present

I study the effects of large-bodied animals on ecosystem carbon cycling dynamics, relying on empirical data collection in natural or experimental settings. I am currently exploring how the introduced moose population on the island of Newfoundland, and its intensive browsing on regenerating boreal tree seedlings, influences forest recovery after disturbances. I am particularly interested in the carbon storage and cycling dynamics across the disturbance and population density gradients, incorporating the soil ecology of forest succession with the continued browsing stress from moose.

Fluxbots

DIY ecological monitoring · 2019–present

As a zoogeochemist, I am interested in measuring, monitoring, and characterizing the carbon cycle across spatial and temporal scales that are relevant to large-bodied animals like moose. However, current tools on the market for measuring (for example) soil carbon respiration are not able to be deployed at these kinds of scales. Since 2017, I have been developing, designing, building, and deploying iterations of the "Fluxbot": an independently-operating, autonomous soil carbon flux chamber that uses inexpensive carbon dioxide and other sensors in combination with store-bought parts to enable large-scale data collection across a distributed array. Fluxbot arrays have been successfully deployed in MA, CA, Kenya, Newfoundland Canada, Brazil, and Palmyra Atoll; stay 'tuned for future deployments in Australia and NY.

Soil Health and Carbon Across Agroecosystems

Hudson, NY and elsewhere · 2024–2026

At Hudson Carbon, I explored questions around the effects of farm management on soil health, soil carbon and nutrient cycling, the capacity of farms to act as reservoirs for atmospheric carbon through increased soil carbon sequestration, and the resilience of farmscapes in the face of increasingly hot, dry conditions. We interrogated questions like: How does farm system complexity (ecological to economical) contribute to farm resistance and resilience to disturbances like drought, market disruption, and more? We relied on a collaborating network of farmers in the Hudson Valley, and conducting truly participatory research that is shaped by the needs and interests of farmers themselves.
