Research

How do ecological communities respond to environmental change — and what does that mean for the people who depend on them?

My research sits at the intersection of animal community ecology and biogeochemistry -- an emergent field of study referred to as zoogeochemistry. I use a combination of long-term field observation, manipulative experiments, and quantitative modeling to ask how ecosytems respond to change in the animal communities that inhabit them, whether that change is through species loss, species introduction, or the addition or subtraction of domestic animals. I'm particularly interested in how changes to an animal community interact with ecosystem structure, and thus its function -- like carbon cycling, biodiversity, and the networks of interactions between animal species. My work has taken me to tropical savannas and atolls, boreal and temperate forests, agricultural landscapes, and marine intertidal zones.

Current projects

Soil Health and Carbon Across Agroecosystems

Hudson, NY and elsewhere · 2024–present

At Hudson Carbon, I work with a team of collaborators to explore questions around the effects of farm management on soil health, soil carbon and nitrogen cycling, the capacity of farms to act as reservoirs for atmospheric carbon through increased soil carbon sequestration, and the resilience of farmscapes in the face of increasingly hot, dry conditions. We also interrogate other questions: how does the addition of sea kelp into the diets of dairy cows influence the methane emissions they contribute to, from the feeding trough to the field? How does farm system complexity (from ecological to economical) contribute to farm resistance and resilience to disturbances like drought, market disruption, and more?
